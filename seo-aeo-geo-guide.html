SEO, AEO & GEO: a practical guide to modern discoverability

Search is no longer only a list of blue links. People ask questions in search engines, AI assistants, social platforms, and answer tools. The goal is still the same: make genuinely useful information easy to find and understand.

Start with the shared foundation

SEO, AEO, and GEO are connected practices, not three unrelated checklists. They all depend on a healthy website: useful pages, clear information architecture, fast loading, accessible markup, accurate facts, and content that helps a visitor. If those basics are weak, extra optimisation language will not create durable visibility.

SEO helps pages appear in search results. AEO helps systems extract a direct answer. GEO helps generative systems understand, trust, and reference information. The best work starts with the user’s need and makes the answer clear enough for many discovery systems to use.

What SEO still means

Search engine optimisation helps search engines crawl, interpret, and rank a page for relevant queries. It includes site structure, indexing controls, canonical URLs, redirects, structured data where appropriate, mobile experience, performance, internal linking, useful titles, logical headings, topical depth, and evidence.

SEO is not just keywords

Keywords matter because they reveal the language people use, but a page is not useful because a phrase is repeated. A strong page answers the reason behind the query. Someone searching for WordPress performance optimisation may want a diagnosis, implementation support, or a checklist. The page should make its audience, promise, process, and next step obvious.

A helpful SEO test: can a visitor understand what this page offers, who it is for, and what to do next within a few seconds?

Technical priorities

  • Make important pages discoverable with internal links and a correct XML sitemap.
  • Use one clear topic with a descriptive title and meaningful H1.
  • Keep headings logical and use them to organise real information.
  • Optimise images, scripts, caching, and delivery for real devices.
  • Use Google Search Console to see indexing, queries, clicks, and issues.

What AEO adds

Answer Engine Optimisation makes content easy for answer-oriented systems to extract and present. This includes featured snippets, voice interfaces, assistant results, and search experiences that answer a question before a visitor clicks. AEO rewards direct, well-structured explanations.

Write the answer before the expansion

When a heading asks a real question, begin with a concise answer. Then explain the detail, limitations, examples, and next steps. This is clearer for people and easier for answer systems to interpret. A good FAQ answers the question in the first sentence rather than postponing the useful information below marketing language.

What GEO adds

Generative Engine Optimisation is an emerging practice around how AI systems retrieve, synthesise, and reference information. There is no reliable shortcut that guarantees an AI mention. The durable qualities are original knowledge, transparent authorship, current facts, clear citations where relevant, well-organised pages, and a consistent entity presence across the web.

Build content worth citing

A shallow page that repeats common advice gives a system little reason to use it. A useful page explains its method, includes concrete examples, distinguishes fact from opinion, names the author or company, and links to primary evidence when appropriate. For a business, this can mean useful service detail, approved case studies, honest buyer answers, and consistent company information.

One workflow for SEO, AEO, and GEO

Use one disciplined workflow rather than three parallel workstreams. First understand the business goal and the user’s question. Audit technical foundations: pages, crawlability, performance, mobile behaviour, internal links, and tracking. Improve page structure so headings create a logical journey. Write direct answers and expand them with useful context. Then measure what changes.

  1. Choose the page’s job. Educate, convert, support, compare, or answer a specific question.
  2. Map the intent. Look at the questions, terms, and related needs behind the topic.
  3. Make the structure visible. Use an honest title, one H1, logical H2s, descriptive links, and accessible media.
  4. Improve speed and usability. A slow or confusing page undermines every form of discoverability.

Measure and improve

Launching is not the end of optimisation. Performance data, Search Console, analytics, real user behaviour, and technical audits show what needs attention next. Do not change everything at once. Make a prioritised change, document it, and observe the outcome. This builds a reliable improvement loop instead of random activity.

Trust is the long-term advantage

Do not publish AI-generated pages without review, evidence, and a real point of view. Do not add FAQ schema when the page lacks genuine questions and answers. Do not chase every acronym while ignoring broken links, missing titles, poor mobile behaviour, or unclear conversion paths. The most sustainable strategy is to make each important page demonstrably useful.

SEO, AEO, and GEO are different lenses on the same responsibility: publishing information that is useful, structured, fast, credible, and easy to understand. Build for people first, make the technical foundation strong, and improve with evidence.
